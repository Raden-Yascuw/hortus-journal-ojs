Optimization of Biochar-Enriched Organic Ameliorants on Peat Soil Nutrient Retention and Oil Palm (Elaeis guineensis) Seedling Vigour

Enhancing Cation Exchange Capacity and Phosphorus Availability in Tropical Sapric Peatlands

Authors

  • Ahmad Kamal Mohd. NoorFaculty of Agriculture, Universiti Putra Malaysia (UPM), Serdanghttps://orcid.org/0000-0002-7701-5512
  • Nayla SyukurillahDepartemen Agrosains & Pusat Kajian Bioteknologi Tropika, Universitas Negeri Malanghttps://orcid.org/0000-0002-1564-0003
  • Arif BudimanDepartemen Ilmu Tanah dan Sumberdaya Lahan, IPB University, Bogorhttps://orcid.org/0000-0003-2901-7710

DOI:

https://doi.org/10.17977/um.hj.v1i1.2026.04

Keywords:

Biochar, Tropical Peatland, Elaeis guineensis, Nutrient Retention, Cation Exchange Capacity

Abstract

Acidic tropical peat soils suffer from severe phosphorus (P) and potassium (K) leaching, resulting in high fertilizer losses during the main-nursery stage of oil palm (Elaeis guineensis Jacq.). This study optimized empty fruit bunch (EFB)-derived biochar enriched with humic compost at ratios of 0%, 5%, 10%, and 15% (w/w) in a 9-month main-nursery experiment across Riau and Selangor peat substrates. Amending sapric peat with 10% EFB-biochar increased soil pH from 3.72 to 5.18, elevated effective cation exchange capacity (ECEC) by 46.2%, and reduced nitrate and K⁺ leaching losses by 38.9% compared to unamended NPK controls (p < 0.001). Consequently, Dickson Quality Index (DQI) of Tenera seedlings improved by 34.5%, establishing an environmentally sound nursery fertilization protocol for sustainable peatland management.
