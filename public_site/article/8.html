Controlled Fermentation Kinetics Using Indigenous Lactobacillus plantarum and Saccharomyces cerevisiae to Enhance Flavor Precursors of Fine Robusta and Liberica Beans

Metabolomic Profiling of Volatile Esters, Organic Acids, and Specialty Cupping Scores

Authors

  • Bagus SetiawanPusat Penelitian Kopi dan Kakao Indonesia (ICCRI), Jemberhttps://orcid.org/0000-0003-1122-4401

DOI:

Keywords:

Postharvest Fermentation, Lactobacillus plantarum, Saccharomyces cerevisiae, Coffea liberica, Flavor Metabolomics

Abstract

Spontaneous mucilage fermentation in Robusta (Coffea canephora) and Liberica (Coffea liberica) processing frequently yields inconsistent sensory quality and excessive bitter-phenolic notes. This study investigated controlled co-inoculation kinetics using indigenous starter cultures of Lactobacillus plantarum (10⁷ CFU/mL) and Saccharomyces cerevisiae (10⁶ CFU/mL) over 36, 48, and 60 hours at 28 °C in anaerobic bioreactors. Sugar consumption, organic acid accumulation (HPLC), and volatile ester generation (HS-SPME-GC-MS) were correlated with Q-Grader sensory evaluations. Co-inoculation for 48 hours accelerated mucilage pectin degradation by 35%, increased fruity ethyl-acetate and isoamyl-acetate esters by 2.6-fold, and elevated SCA/CQI fine cupping scores from 78.25 (spontaneous control) to 84.75 for Fine Robusta and 85.10 for Liberica (p < 0,001).
